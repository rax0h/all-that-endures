# Current Simulation Standard

The simulation models civilization as consequences distributed across people, relationships, practices, institutions, objects and places. Culture is not a personality tag and species is not culture.

## Causal composition

Embodied/species conditions + rank + geography + resources + weather + needs + relationships + accumulated knowledge + historical events create pressures. People and institutions respond. Responses become practices, techniques, laws, property arrangements, infrastructure and traditions. Those are transmitted imperfectly and feed the next generation.

## Required distinctions

- capability is not consequence; rank creates capability while history creates impact
- species biology is not personality, morality or culture
- culture is reconstructed from behavior, practices, knowledge, objects and institutions rather than stored as a monolithic civilization trait
- objective events, memories, beliefs and narratives remain distinct
- property and artifacts preserve provenance
- relationships preserve shared history
- births preserve genealogy
- techniques may branch, mutate, hybridize, spread and disappear
- law requires rules plus enforcement and legitimacy
- institutions have continuity beyond individual officeholders
- settlements must physically accommodate their actual populations; architectural style arises historically
- historical probability never changes because the player may later encounter something

## Cultural-evolution surface

The target engine permits regional and overlapping differences in construction, food/cuisine, preservation, agriculture, medicine, clothing, aesthetics, weapons, warfare, law, inheritance, kinship, education, religion/philosophy, language/dialect, measurement, trade standards, infrastructure, property, craft, technology, magic practice, governance, diplomacy and social norms. Differences must be attributable to transmission, environment, embodied constraints, resources, institutions, contact or historical contingency.

## Acceptance test

Remove culture labels. Given the resulting people, practices, institutions, laws, objects, language/knowledge and places, recognizable cultural patterns must still be recoverable. If not, culture has been implemented as tags rather than history.
